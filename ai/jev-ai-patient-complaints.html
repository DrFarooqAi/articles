THE CORE IDEA

From monthly reports to same-day escalation

Complaint tagging is not a judgment call that needs a specialist. It is a fixed, repeatable set of questions: which department, what theme, how serious, is anyone at risk. That is exactly the kind of small, well-scoped question Jev is built to answer, and it answers every complaint the same way, in seconds, instead of however a tired staff member reads it at 4pm on a Thursday.

The category list and the escalation rules stay in your own code, not inside a model. When management decides billing errors need their own theme, or a new department opens, someone edits a list. Nobody retrains anything.

HOW THE QUESTIONS MAP

Three tag types, one complaint

Take a real one: "Waited 4 hours in ER, my father's pain medicine was given late, and then we were charged for a test that was never done." One call to Jev can tag all of it at once, and each tag type does a different job.

CHOICE

Categorization

Department (ER, OPD, inpatient, billing, pharmacy, radiology) and theme (waiting time, staff attitude, communication, billing error, clinical care, facilities, appointment access). Complaints often carry more than one theme, so each gets asked as its own question.

SCORE

Severity & Emotion

Severity on a 1 to 5 scale, and patient emotion from calm to very angry. This is what lets a dashboard show rising heat in one clinic before it becomes a pattern anyone notices by hand.

NOUL (TRUE/FALSE)

Red Flags

Possible harm to a patient, mentions of legal action or media, an insurance denial, a specific staff member named, or the complaint actually being a compliment. Each flag is its own yes or no, so nothing gets buried inside a single tag.

For the ER example above, Jev's answers route the complaint without anyone reading it first: the harm flag sends it to patient safety today, and the billing flag opens a ticket with revenue cycle in parallel.

ROUTING BY CONFIDENCE AND RISK

Safety flags move the same day, no exceptions

Everything else can wait for a batch job. A possible harm flag, a legal or media mention, cannot, and the routing logic treats that as a hard rule rather than a suggestion Jev can talk itself out of.

BOTTOM LINE
This is a lower-risk starting point than an audit tool. The decisions here are not financial or clinical, the payoff shows up to leadership within weeks, and past complaint data gives you ready-made ground truth to test against.

    


    

      
Start with 500 cases, not a full rollout

      
Pull three to six months of complaints your team already tagged by hand. Run 500 de-identified cases through Jev, then compare department, theme, severity and the safety flag against what your staff wrote. Run Arabic and English separately so language differences show up clearly, measure the agreement rate and how many safety cases it actually caught, and let that pilot decide whether Jev earns a wider rollout.
